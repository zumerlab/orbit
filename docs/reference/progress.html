Progress

<o-progress> fills a lane up to a value, over a track that shows the whole range. It is the base of progress rings, gauges and meters.

A progress goes straight into a ring, one per ring: put each bar of a multi-bar meter on its own ring. It is drawn by the runtime, so it needs the Orbit script. For screen readers, give it role="progressbar" and the aria- values, as above.

Value and color

value runs from 0 to max (100 by default) and fills that share of the ring's range. --o-fill colors the bar and --o-back-fill the track behind it; --o-stroke and --o-back-stroke outline them.

--o-progress sets the value from CSS and wins over the attribute. Use it when a class or a parent owns the state:

Orbit redraws when the element's or an ancestor's class or style changes. After a change it cannot see, such as editing a stylesheet rule, call Orbit.refresh(). The value does not animate: a transition on --o-progress jumps straight to the new value.

Endings

shape sets how the bar ends: circle (or circle-a), circle-b, bullet, arrow, slash, backslash, rounded and zigzag. Endings are sized from the band, so they look the same on every ring and at every size.

Stroke variant

By default the bar is a filled band. For a thin gauge line, add variant="stroke": the bar and the track become lines along the middle of the lane, with round caps.

  • --o-stroke colors the line and --o-back-stroke the track. --o-fill and --o-back-fill are not used.
  • The line is 2px wide (--o-stroke-width) and the track 1px (--o-back-stroke-width), in screen pixels at any size.
  • The track is gray by default; set --o-back-stroke to color it, or none to hide it.
  • shape does not apply. A full value on a 360° range draws a closed circle.

Thickness and position

A progress is one lane thick. Set --o-size-ratio in lanes on the element, its ring or the .gravity-spot, or use the .grow-* and .shrink-* classes on the element. .inner-orbit and .outer-orbit move the band in or out so one of its edges sits on the lane, and the .quarter- versions go part of the way. A band two lanes thick on orbit-11 ends exactly at the edge of the universe.

Pointer interaction

<o-progress> is decorative by default, so it never steals clicks from controls nearby. interactive makes the whole track clickable, including the part the bar does not cover, whatever its colors. hit-width adds touch area in px on each side, which helps with thin bars on touch screens.

When a progress acts as a control, you bring the keyboard behavior and the accessible name. It is placed with the rotate property, so your own transform or scale adds to it instead of replacing it.

After drawing

Each <o-progress> fires a bubbling o-rendered event after it redraws. To wait for everything on the page, for example before measuring, use await Orbit.ready(). See Runtime and resizing.

Satellites

Satellites travel the orbits. A .satellite sits on a ring and carries your content in a .capsule; Orbit spreads all the satellites of a ring evenly around it.

A satellite is a circle one lane across, with no border or background until you give it one. Put content in a .capsule inside it, never directly in the satellite: the capsule keeps it centered and upright. See Capsules.

Size

Set --o-size-ratio, in lanes, on the satellite, its ring or the .gravity-spot. For one satellite there are also classes, which win over any ratio set around them:

  • .shrink-5 to .shrink-100 make it smaller than a lane: shrink-30 is 0.7 lanes.
  • .grow-1x to .grow-12x make it 2 to 24 lanes. Decimals add fifths of a lane: grow-1.2x is 2.4 lanes.
  • .grow-0.1x to .grow-0.9x add tenths of a lane: grow-0.5x is 1.5 lanes.

Shape and look

Satellites are round. .box makes one square and .rounded-box a square with soft corners. Anything else is plain CSS: backgrounds, borders, images, SVG.

Position on the lane

A satellite is centered on its lane. .inner-orbit moves it in so its outer edge touches the lane, and .outer-orbit moves it out so its inner edge does. .quarter-inner-orbit and .quarter-outer-orbit move it part of the way. For any other offset, set --o-alignment: a positive length moves it in, a negative one out.

An exact angle

.angle-0 to .angle-360 place a satellite at that angle, clockwise from the top, instead of its turn on the ring. It ignores the ring's --o-from.

Facing the center

A satellite keeps its content upright wherever it is. With .spin-lock it turns with its angle instead, so the top of its content always points at the center, like the labels on a wheel. Add .flip to the capsule to make it point outward. Left, the default; right, spin-lock.

Which way is out

Each satellite exposes --o-dir-x and --o-dir-y: the direction from the center of its system to the satellite, from -1 to 1. x grows to the right and y grows down, so a satellite at the top has 0 and -1, and a .satellite.at-center has both at 0. Its content inherits them, so CSS can push a label outward whatever the angle:

For labels there are two classes. Put either on a capsule, or on an element inside one. .outward sets it just outside the satellite along its direction, with its nearest edge or corner --o-outward-gap (4px) away. .outward-y only goes up or down: above the satellites of the upper half and below the rest, so long labels stay inside a narrow screen.

On the value of a progress

A .satellite.at-value sits on the value of the <o-progress> in its ring and follows it: the knob of a dial. See A satellite on the value.

A satellite is a universe

Put a .gravity-spot directly inside a satellite and it reaches the satellite's edge: a moon system around a planet, a gauge inside a menu item. Make the satellite thicker to give it room. See Gravity spot.

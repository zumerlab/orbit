Arcs

<o-arc> draws a piece of a lane, with text along it if you want. Sibling arcs split their ring between them, or stack one after another by value.

Arcs go straight into a ring, next to satellites or vectors if you like. They are drawn by the runtime, so they need the Orbit script.

Split or stack

Without value, sibling arcs share the ring equally: a segmented ring, or a pie when they are thick. With value, each takes its share of the range and starts where the previous one ended: a donut chart. Values are not normalized, so make them add up to max for a full ring. With max="24", value="6" is a quarter. An explicit value="0" draws nothing and takes no room.

Color

--o-fill colors the band, and --o-stroke and --o-stroke-width its outline. They change with a short transition, so a hover state is one rule. Hover needs interactive: arcs ignore the pointer until you ask.

Endings

shape sets how each end looks: circle (or circle-a), circle-b, bullet, arrow, slash, backslash, rounded and zigzag. Endings are sized from the band, so they look the same on every ring and at every size.

Thickness, position and gaps

An arc is one lane thick. Set --o-size-ratio in lanes on the arc, its ring or the .gravity-spot, or use the .grow-* and .shrink-* classes on the arc. .inner-orbit and .outer-orbit move the band in or out so one of its edges sits on the lane, and the .quarter- versions go part of the way.

--o-gap is the space left at each end of an arc. It cascades, so one value on the ring spaces all its arcs; .gap-0 to .gap-30 set it on one arc. A ring of 3 to 6 thick segments usually wants --o-gap between 24 and 60 to read as separate pieces.

Arcs follow the ring's --o-range, --o-from and .ccw (see Orbits). .angle-N on an arc starts it at that angle instead of its turn on the ring.

Curved text

Text inside an <o-arc> is drawn along it. Style it like any text: color, font-size, font-weight, text-transform. The font size is the same in px on every ring. Set --o-fill: transparent for text with no band.

text-anchor places the text at the start, middle (default) or end of its arc.

Pointer interaction

Arcs are decorative by default, so they never steal clicks from controls nearby. interactive makes the whole band clickable, even where its fill is transparent, and hit-width adds touch area in px on each side, which helps with thin arcs on touch screens.

When an arc acts as a control, you bring the keyboard behavior and the accessible name. Arcs are placed with the rotate property, so your own transform or scale (on hover, say) adds to it instead of replacing it.

After drawing

Each <o-arc> fires a bubbling o-rendered event after it redraws. To wait for every arc on the page, for example before measuring, use await Orbit.ready(). See Runtime and resizing.

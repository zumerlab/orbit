IoT and hardware interfaces

Devices are full of dials: thermostats, fan speeds, fuel and battery levels, valve positions. The quantities are continuous and bounded, so a radial control matches how people think of the thing. Orbit gives you those controls as HTML that any framework, or none, can update from a WebSocket or MQTT message.

Where it fits

  • Smart-home panels and cards that should look the same across platforms.
  • Companion apps for devices with a knob or a gauge of their own: e-bikes, printers, brewing controllers.
  • Device screens prototyped in HTML, then shipped in a web view.
  • Live telemetry: one attribute or custom property per message, redrawn on the next frame.

Where it does not

  • Old embedded browsers. Orbit needs container query units and CSS sin() and cos().
  • Illustrated, branded controls. Draw the illustration and put an Orbit gauge on top for the values.

A thermostat screen could be a large temperature gauge, a knob for the setpoint and two small rings for humidity and filter life. The Kit instrument and gauge are good starting points.
